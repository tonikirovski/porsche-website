Abstract
(use up to 150 words to describe what was the motivation and the result of this thesis)
================================================================================

This thesis presents porsche-website, an Angular 19 web application that implements an educational
Porsche-style digital showroom. The objective is to determine how fully browsing, comparison, and
configuration can operate through client-side rendering and static data, and where production
systems would introduce inventory records and authenticated users.

The solution provides a home page with promotional media and an embedded dealership map, fourteen
catalog variants with filters for model line, fuel type, and transmission, detailed variant pages, and
two-vehicle comparison. A JSON-backed configurator adjusts imagery and live pricing for exterior
colors, wheels, interior finishes, seats, technology, and equipment. Applicable convertibles add
roof-state handling. EmailJS handles quote requests. jsPDF and html2canvas create downloadable
configuration PDFs. A dedicated route presents brand content. Inventory backends and account sign-in
stay out of scope. Data modeling, navigation, animations, and external services are therefore realized
entirely on the client.


================================================================================
INTRODUCTION
================================================================================

High-involvement products such as premium sports cars are researched heavily online before any
dealership visit. Stakeholders therefore judge a brand as much by the quality of its digital showroom—
layout, motion, clarity of flows, and perceived “finish”—as by the raw feasibility of the underlying
code. This thesis is anchored in that user-facing layer: an educational, non-official Porsche-style
web application named porsche-website, implemented as a single-page Angular 19 front end that a
reviewer can run locally or from static hosting.

Analytics on typical web sessions suggest that visitors spend only a short time on an individual
page before they scroll, navigate away, or commit to reading further; aggregate figures in industry
reporting often sit around six seconds for many landing and content pages. That constraint frames
both experience design and engineering: the surface must be intriguing enough to earn attention,
yet typographic hierarchy, spacing, and predictable navigation must keep dense technical and pricing
information legible. A premium automotive site compounds the challenge because it stacks marketing
media, catalogue discovery, long-form specifications, comparison, and configuration in one journey.

The delivered application, porsche-website, explores how far that journey can be realised without a
custom inventory or customer backend. Client-side routing (Angular Router) structures the main flows:
a home route; a models catalogue at /models; variant detail at /models/:model/:variant; configuration
at /configure/:variant; comparison at /compare; and brand storytelling at /brand, with unknown paths
falling back to the home route. Fourteen illustrative variants are defined in a central TypeScript
catalogue (911, 718, Taycan, Panamera, Macan, and Cayenne families) with filters for model line, fuel
type, and transmission. Each configurable variant loads its options, imagery, and price deltas from
an imported JSON document; the configurator component maps the route parameter to the correct file,
updates hero imagery (including a touch-friendly image carousel), and keeps a running price in sync
with selections. Convertible-capable trims (911 Cabriolet and 718 Spyder RS) add explicit roof-state
logic so exterior and interior previews stay consistent with open or closed roof. The home route
combines an introductory video treatment, a draggable model-line carousel, and an embedded Google Map
for dealership context. Elsewhere, a quote form is sent through EmailJS; a configuration summary is
exported with jsPDF and html2canvas; Angular animations support expandable sections and modals; and
scroll-triggered reveals on the brand route use IntersectionObserver.

The repository also includes production-oriented scripts—for example a GitHub Pages build with an
appropriate base href—so the same artefact can be discussed as a deployable static bundle.

The thesis does not implement manufacturer order management, live stock, regional pricing rules, or
authenticated user accounts. The written work uses that boundary deliberately: it separates what a
client-rendered showroom can demonstrate today from the server-side systems a production deployment
would require.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Problem Statement
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Beyond first impressions, premium automotive sites compress a long decision into one session:
marketing-led home content, a structured model range, filtered catalogues, technical disclosure,
comparison, and a multi-step configurator where imagery and price must stay aligned. From a software
perspective the difficulty is not only to render each screen, but to keep catalogue, detail, compare,
and configure views consistent so users trust what they see. That coherence problem becomes sharper
when the implementation deliberately avoids inventory databases and authenticated accounts, because
there is no server-side source of truth for stock or customer-specific offers.

This thesis addresses that gap through porsche-website. The concrete question is how much of the
end-to-end journey—browsing fourteen illustrative variants, comparing two vehicles, and configuring
options with live feedback—can be carried by client-side rendering, static TypeScript and JSON data,
and selected third-party services (maps, transactional email, PDF export), and where production
systems would normally insert inventory checks, pricing rules tied to region or identity, and saved
user profiles. Motivation for the work is therefore both engineering and evaluative: to build a
demonstrably complete front end, and to document where the client-only assumption stops being
realistic for a commercial deployment.

A short historical note suffices for context: single-vendor automotive configurators moved from
desktop tools and dealership kiosks toward the web as broadband and imagery improved; today they are
a standard part of lead generation. This project does not recreate a manufacturer back office; it
isolates the shopper-facing layer so the thesis can discuss architecture, interaction design, and
integration boundaries without conflating them with enterprise IT.

Outline of the Thesis
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The document opens with the abstract and this introduction. Together they fix terminology
(educational Porsche-style showroom, client-only data), the user-experience motivation (short
attention windows, readability, coherence across flows), and the capstone question stated in the
problem statement above.

Following the introduction, a background or related-work style chapter typically situates Angular
single-page applications, common automotive e-commerce and configurator patterns, and approaches to
static or JSON-driven product data on the client. A requirements chapter then derives functional and
non-functional expectations from the routes and behaviours actually implemented: home media and map,
catalogue filtering, variant detail and technical panels, two-vehicle comparison with modal
pickers, the multi-section configurator (including roof-dependent imagery where applicable), email
and PDF touchpoints, brand content, responsiveness, and error-tolerant routing.

The analytical core of the thesis describes information architecture (URL scheme, navigation shell,
relationship between the shared VARIANTS catalogue and per-variant configuration JSON), configurator
state management, image selection rules, and handling of edge cases such as unknown routes or
missing configuration keys. The implementation narrative is organised by feature area rather than by
file listing, mirroring the application’s modules: home, models, variant, compare, car-config, and
brand, plus cross-cutting concerns (animations, forms, third-party APIs, build and deployment).

Later sections cover verification and honesty about limits: unit tests where present, manual test
passes, known simplifications (illustrative prices, no real CRM handoff), and a forward-looking note
on what a minimal backend would change. The thesis concludes with outcomes against objectives,
trade-offs of the client-only approach, and suggestions for future work. Optional appendices can hold
extended figures, JSON excerpts, screenshots, or a demonstration checklist without interrupting the
main argument.
